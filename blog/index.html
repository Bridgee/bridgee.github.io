About Me

Welcome to my digital realm! My name is Zhouqiao Zhao, and I go by Bridge. In August 2026, I joined Loyola Marymount University (LMU), as an Assistant Professor of Electrical and Computer Engineering, where I lead the HAITS Lab—Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence for Intelligent Transportation Systems.

My research develops human-centered AI that connects how people behave, react, decide, and interact with the design of intelligent agents, fleets, transportation networks, and system-level operations. I combine artificial intelligence and machine learning, robotics, control, optimization, human factors, simulation and digital twins, graph neural networks and explainable AI, reinforcement and inverse reinforcement learning, and multimodal and foundation models. The work advances safety, environmental sustainability and mobility, while supporting trustworthy human–AI collaboration and effective transportation operations.

Before joining LMU, I was a Postdoctoral Associate (September 2023–July 2026) working with the MIT AgeLab and MIT Center for Transportation and Logistics. I received my PhD in Electrical Engineering from UC Riverside (2018–2023), where I worked with Matthew Barth and Guoyuan Wu at CE-CERT. My dissertation, A Connected Automation-Enabled Cooperative Management Framework for Mixed Traffic, developed a cooperative management framework for mixed traffic [Dissertation]. I previously earned an MS in Electrical and Computer Engineering from The Ohio State University and a BS in Electronic and Information Engineering from the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China.

Earlier, I conducted industry research with Toyota Motor North America InfoTech Labs and Honda Research Institute. Beyond academia, I enjoy photography, sound design, music composition, and synthesizer design. A current record of my work and experience is available in my CV.

HAITS Lab

Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence for Intelligent Transportation Systems

HAITS (pronounced “heights”) is my research lab at LMU. We study how understanding people can inform the design of intelligent agents and transportation systems—from individual behavior and interaction to fleets, networks, and system-level operations.

Transportation is our primary application domain and proving ground, not the boundary of the research. The lab combines artificial intelligence and machine learning, robotics, control, optimization, human factors, simulation and digital twins, graph neural networks and explainable AI, reinforcement and inverse reinforcement learning, and multimodal and foundation models.

My Research

Vision

My research asks how intelligent systems can be designed around the people who use, supervise, and share space with them. I model how people behave, react, decide, and interact, then translate that understanding into prediction, planning, coordination, and system design.

The agenda spans multiple spatial and temporal scales: human behavior, reactions & interactions → trajectories → mobility choices → intelligent agents & fleets → transportation networks → system-level operations. Transportation is the primary domain in which I develop and test these ideas, rather than the boundary of the research.

The work advances safety, environmental sustainability and mobility, while supporting trustworthy human–AI collaboration and effective transportation operations.

Roadmap & Framework

Human-Centered AI: from behavior and interaction to transportation system design

The framework connects observations of people and context to models of behavior, trajectories, and choices; those models then support intelligent agents, coordinated fleets, transportation networks, and system-level operations. Simulation and digital twins provide a bridge between algorithm development and real-world evaluation.
